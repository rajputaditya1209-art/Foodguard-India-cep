What is Food Adulteration?

Jump to Label Guide ↓

Food adulteration means adding inferior, harmful, unwanted, or unauthorized substances to food, or removing valuable ingredients from food, in a way that reduces its quality or may make it unsafe.

Protect Yourself: Prevention Tips

Check packaging for tampering or damage
Verify FSSAI license numbers and logos
Always check manufacturing and expiry dates
Buy from reliable, licensed sellers
Avoid suspiciously cheap products without labels
Keep purchase receipts/bills for future reference

Verify Before You Buy: Package Label Guide

Every legally sold packaged food in India must carry the FSSAI logo and a 14-digit license number. Checking this takes just a few seconds and should be your first step before buying.

How to Verify Online

You can check the 14-digit license number on the official FoSCoS portal to confirm it is real and active.

Verify License at FoSCoS Portal →

Red Flags to Watch Out For

  • Missing FSSAI logo or license number entirely.
  • License number is not exactly 14 digits.
  • License number or dates are blurry, hand-written, or pasted as a separate loose sticker.
  • Missing Batch Number or Manufacturing/Expiry dates.

Report Food Adulteration

If you suspect that a food product is unsafe, adulterated, misbranded, or violates food-safety requirements, report it immediately to the official food-safety authority.
